03 Compare the scope

Not every household
needs whole-home backup.

A focused approach starts with priority loads—especially refrigeration—and can also consider internet and media-center equipment. Traditional residential solar is primarily a generation and offset conversation; outage resilience depends on a configuration designed to operate safely when grid power is unavailable. The right scope depends on the home, the loads, the site, the equipment, and the local requirements.

Scope first: these are directional differences, not universal cost, timing, or portability claims. Each household and project needs its own review.

Focused priority loads

SunChill household resilience

A focused resilience approach for the loads a household cares about most, with refrigeration first and solar support considered alongside backup priorities.

Financial review
By focusing on priority loads, the concept explores a different scope than whole-home backup. Any cost comparison requires like-for-like quotes and project details.
Relocation
A portable-minded approach may be easier to relocate than a permanently integrated system; a new location still needs its own fit and code review.
Project path
The concept is designed to keep the scope focused—not to skip the steps that make a household energy system safe.

Broader electrical scope

Whole-home battery backup

Whole-home backup covers a broader set of household circuits and may involve transfer equipment, service-panel work, and a larger integrated project scope.

Financial review
Broader load coverage and electrical integration create a different project scope. Cost and value depend on equipment, labor, permits, site conditions, and other project details.
Relocation
Permanently mounted or electrically integrated equipment is generally tied more closely to the property than equipment designed for relocation.
Project path
Design, electrical work, permits, inspections, and on-site coordination vary by the home, system, contractor, and jurisdiction.

Generation and offset

Traditional residential solar

Traditional residential solar primarily addresses generation and grid offset. Solar offset and outage backup are different jobs, and resilience requires a configuration designed for grid outages.

Financial review
Bill impact, payback, and lifecycle cost vary with load, weather, orientation, shading, utility rules, equipment, and use.
Relocation
Property-mounted solar is generally designed to remain with the site. Any move or reinstallation requires a new technical and local review.
Project path
If solar PV is included, the applicable design, electrical, structural, permitting, and utility paths must be confirmed for the project location.

Permitting and electrical requirements vary by configuration and location. If solar PV, fixed wiring, structural mounting, or interconnection is involved, the applicable review and permit path must be confirmed before work begins. No performance, savings, installation-timing, or availability promise is made here.

07 Questions

Careful
answers.

What is SunChill exploring?

SunChill is exploring a household essential-load resilience approach centered on refrigerator backup power and solar offset.

Internet and media-center backup and offset are secondary areas of focus.

Is this a whole-home concept?

No. The exploration is intentionally limited to selected household loads rather than the whole home.

Refrigeration is primary; internet and media-center loads are secondary.

Does solar offset mean a specific bill result?

No. Solar offset describes a direction to explore, not a prediction about a household bill.

Sunlight and energy-use patterns vary, so this page makes no financial claim.

What does “backup power” mean here?

It refers to exploring stored energy as support for selected loads when the grid is disrupted.

The phrase does not state continuous operation or a particular outcome.

What household details matter to the discussion?

Useful context can include refrigerator energy use, which secondary loads matter to you, local sunlight, and the kinds of grid disruptions you are thinking about.

Sharing context starts a conversation; it does not establish a conclusion.

What is not being claimed?

This page does not claim a particular outcome, continuous power, a financial result, or whole-home coverage.

It presents a focused idea and the boundaries around it.

What happens when I email?

You can share a question or a little household context. The purpose is simply to exchange information about the idea.

You do not need to fill out a form or make any decision.
